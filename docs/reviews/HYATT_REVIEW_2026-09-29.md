## Directly supported high-confidence claims

From NIST's public case summary:
- the second- and fourth-floor suspended walkways collapsed on 17 July 1981;
- 113 people were killed and 186 injured;
- NBS was asked to determine the most probable cause;
- NBS concluded the most probable cause was insufficient load capacity of the box beam-hanger rod connections;
- observed distortions strongly suggested initiation at the east end of the fourth-floor walkway's middle box beam connection.

## Editorial change

The earlier draft moved too quickly from “the arrangement changed” to a general load-path lesson. The new version leads with NBS's explicit cause finding, explains load-path reasoning without inventing a structural calculation, and quarantines the famous “doubled load” shorthand until the exact connection, geometry and report pages are manually checked.

## Why the numerical detail remains open

BSS 143 contains the detailed comparison of the original and constructed hanger-rod arrangements. The final public story should use those numbers only after a structural reviewer checks the exact pages/figures and confirms that the simplified explanation preserves the actual connection behavior.
