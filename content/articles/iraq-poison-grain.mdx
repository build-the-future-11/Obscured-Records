Farmers and their families developed mercury poisoning after consuming treated grain in Iraq in 1971–1972. A 1976 account by K. Al-Tikriti and A. W. Al-Mufti records 6,530 hospital admissions and 459 deaths among those admitted.

## What happened

The authors also describe people with milder symptoms treated as outpatients. Their hospital figures therefore do not count everyone affected. The abstract identifies a delay of up to sixty days between consumption and symptoms as probably a major contributor to the outbreak’s scale.

## Why this record matters

The hospital denominator and the delay before illness are central to interpreting the figures. The abstract does not establish shipment timing, warning languages or what individual families understood. Those questions require the full study and separate distribution records.
