Residents near Lake Nyos in north-west Cameroon heard a rumble. By morning, people and animals lay dead across the valleys while buildings and vegetation remained largely untouched.

## What happened

Scientists concluded that carbon dioxide had accumulated in the deep volcanic lake and was suddenly released. Heavier than air, the gas flowed downhill and displaced breathable oxygen. The event became known as a limnic eruption.

## Why this record matters

Nyos expanded the boundaries of what communities and scientists understood a lake could do. Degassing pipes now reduce the buildup, but the disaster remains a warning about hazards that leave almost no visual signature until after they have passed.
