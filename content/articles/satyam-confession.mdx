On 7 January 2009, Satyam filed a company announcement and chairman B. Ramalinga Raju’s resignation letter with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. In the letter, Raju described ₹5,040 crore in non-existent cash and bank balances, ₹376 crore in non-existent accrued interest, a ₹1,230 crore understated liability and ₹490 crore in overstated debtors as of 30 September 2008. Those figures are admissions in the letter, not an independent audit.

## What happened

Raju wrote that Satyam had reported September-quarter revenue of ₹2,700 crore and an operating margin of ₹649 crore, compared with what he described as actual revenue of ₹2,112 crore and an actual operating margin of ₹61 crore. He said the balance-sheet gap had grown over several years and described the aborted Maytas acquisition as an attempt to replace fictitious assets with real ones.

A later SEBI adjudication order records the acquisition announcement on 16 December 2008, its cancellation on 17 December and Raju’s letter on 7 January 2009. On 9 January, the Company Law Board suspended the existing board and authorized the central government to constitute a new one. In 2011, the SEC alleged that former senior officials had used false invoices and forged bank statements to overstate revenue, income and cash by more than $1 billion over five years. Satyam settled that action without admitting or denying the allegations.

## Why this record matters

The records answer different questions. Raju’s letter documents his admissions, the SEBI and Company Law Board materials document chronology and intervention, and the SEC action states later enforcement allegations and settlement terms. None should be treated as a complete adjudication of every director’s, auditor’s or employee’s responsibility.
