A room offers more than floor area. In an emergency, it offers a limited amount of usable time. Whether that time is enough depends on what happens to the room and what people can do while it changes.

The 2003 fire at The Station nightclub in Rhode Island killed 100 people. NIST investigated the fire, including the ignition of foam by pyrotechnics, the development of conditions inside the building and evacuation. Its technical work matters because it permits a more specific discussion than the claim that a crowd simply panicked.

## A route on paper is not an evacuation

An exit count is a static description. Escape is a process. People have to recognize danger, find a route, reach it and move through it while visibility, heat and available space may be changing. These steps can overlap, and the slowest constraint can govern the whole process.

That distinction should make writers cautious about retrospective instructions. From the position of a reader who already knows the outcome, an alternative door can look obvious. For someone inside a changing room, visibility and familiarity may have been very different. A map does not grant access to another person's perceptions.

It also matters how an investigation establishes its timing. A video, a laboratory experiment and a computer model are different kinds of evidence. A model can test a mechanism under stated assumptions. It cannot become a recording of an event merely because its animation is convincing. Experimental reconstruction is most useful when the reader can see both what was reproduced and what remained uncertain.

The point is not to distrust technical work. It is to describe its strength accurately. A carefully bounded result is more informative than a dramatic reconstruction presented without limits.

## Before anyone needs to decide

The phrase human behavior can quietly move attention away from design. Yet the conditions in which people decide are themselves designed or permitted: the materials around them, the layout, the presence of suppression systems and the way exits are made intelligible. Individual action cannot be assessed fairly while those conditions remain in the background.

A venue's ordinary atmosphere is part of the challenge. People come to listen, watch and gather, not to conduct an inspection. Safety has to exist before visitors are required to understand every relevant hazard. The everyday use of a room should not demand expert knowledge as a condition of survival.

For an archive, this leads to a different kind of closing image. Not the moment of ignition repeated for spectacle, and not an invented account of a victim's last choice. Instead, a floor plan beside a fire-growth account, with the relationship between them explained. The record should show how available time was shaped before the audience ever arrived.
