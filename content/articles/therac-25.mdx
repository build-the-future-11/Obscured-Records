The Therac-25 radiation therapy machine could produce different kinds of treatment beams. Under a rare sequence of fast operator inputs, its software could enter an unsafe state and expose patients to enormous overdoses.

## What happened

Operators initially struggled to believe the machine was responsible. Error messages were cryptic, earlier designs had used physical interlocks, and incidents at different hospitals were not rapidly connected. Six patients were massively overdosed; several later died.

## Why this record matters

Therac-25 is taught as a software failure, but its record is wider: design assumptions, weak incident reporting, overconfidence in reuse and an interface that hid the system’s true state all aligned. Safety was not one missing check; it was a missing system.
