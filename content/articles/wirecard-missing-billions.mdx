When auditors could not verify €1.9 billion supposedly held in trustee accounts, Wirecard admitted the money probably did not exist. Within days, the German payments company filed for insolvency.

## What happened

The collapse followed years of reporting and whistleblower warnings that were met not only with denial but with scrutiny of the journalists and traders raising questions. Regulators treated Wirecard partly as a technology company rather than as the financial institution its risks resembled.

## Why this record matters

Wirecard illustrates the difference between a reported cash balance and independent evidence that the money exists. ESMA’s later review identified specific supervisory deficiencies; a claim about any person’s criminal responsibility requires its own legal record.
