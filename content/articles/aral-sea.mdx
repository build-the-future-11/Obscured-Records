The Soviet Union diverted the rivers feeding the Aral Sea to irrigate cotton and other crops. The sea receded, salinity rose and a major fishing economy collapsed.

## What happened

Ships were stranded on sand. Exposed seabed became a source of salt and contaminated dust. The northern portion later recovered partly after a dam project, while much of the southern basin continued to fragment and disappear.

## Why this record matters

The Aral Sea is often shown as a before-and-after satellite image. The harder record is social: policy turned hydrology into labour, health, migration and memory—and demonstrated that restoration can be both technically possible and politically uneven.
