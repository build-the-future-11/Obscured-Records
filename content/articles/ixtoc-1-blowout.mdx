On 3 June 1979, the exploratory Ixtoc I well blew out in the Bay of Campeche. The platform caught fire and later collapsed around the wellhead, complicating efforts to regain control.

## What happened

NOAA’s incident history says the well continued to release oil until it was capped on 23 March 1980. Its account traces the loss of drilling-mud circulation, the fire, the collapsed platform and the relief-well response. The agency’s ocean-science archive also documents research cruises that studied the spill.

## Why this record matters

Ixtoc I makes the sequence of an offshore emergency visible: a well-control failure, a damaged platform, containment work and later observation. A final capping date is not the end of the record; response decisions and scientific evidence continue after the visible release stops. This is an AI-assisted brief, not original reporting.
