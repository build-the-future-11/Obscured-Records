# Obscured Records — Four-Month Operating Plan

**Window:** 29 September 2026 through 31 January 2027  
**Source of truth:** this repository and GitHub issues  
**Current product state:** the latest reader experience is deployed to the existing owner-only site. Public audience expansion, named editorial approval, newsletter sending, monitoring ownership, backup/restore rehearsal, media provenance clearance, and analytics remain operational decisions or gates. This plan does not mark any of those complete.

## North star

Build Obscured Records into a credible, repeatable publication with four properties:

1. journalism that is worth returning for;
2. an editorial process that distinguishes draft, review, approval and publication;
3. distribution that compounds through search, email, partnerships and direct readership;
4. measurable operating discipline without inventing traffic, sponsor, contributor or editorial claims.

The plan deliberately prioritizes trust and repeat readership over raw article count.

---

## Phase 1 — Relaunch and operating discipline
### 29 September–31 October 2026

### Objectives

- close the remaining public-launch gates that can be closed by engineering or documented operator action;
- establish one editorial calendar and one issue-backed operating board;
- choose the first flagship stories and create a real review queue;
- implement analytics only after a privacy-conscious collection plan is accepted;
- make every public story measurable, distributable and updateable;
- begin a reliable newsletter workflow without claiming delivery until delivery is actually configured and tested.

### Required outputs by 31 October

- immutable, auditable public-launch candidate;
- named release/incident owner and named editorial owner;
- launch selection for the existing public records;
- media/source issues either cleared or explicitly held;
- monitored intake procedure;
- tested rollback/restore procedure;
- analytics decision and, if accepted, a verified implementation;
- four flagship story packages in review or published;
- a weekly editorial calendar covering at least four weeks;
- distribution checklist attached to every approved story;
- newsletter editorial template and issue cadence ready even if sending remains deferred;
- baseline measurements for search, direct, referral, engaged reading and newsletter capture where collection is actually enabled.

### Editorial cadence

Target quality, not volume. The default Month-1 cadence is:

- 3–5 publishable items per week while launch gates and review quality are still being established;
- 1 flagship/deep-dive candidate per week;
- 1 corrections/update review per week;
- 1 editorial retrospective per week.

Only increase cadence after the process demonstrates that sourcing, review, rights and distribution remain reliable.

### Month-1 decision rules

- A green build is not editorial approval.
- A reachable URL is not a fact check.
- A draft in Git is not a published story.
- A stored newsletter signup is not a delivered newsletter.
- Historical/self-reported audience numbers remain historical until current analytics verify current traffic.
- Do not publish a draft merely to meet a count target.

## Phase 2 — Distribution engine
### 1–30 November 2026

### Objectives

Create repeatable acquisition loops around work that has already passed the editorial quality gate.

### Search

Build 8–12 durable topic clusters from subjects where Obscured Records can provide depth rather than chase generic news. Each cluster should have:

- one pillar page or flagship;
- supporting records;
- intentional internal links;
- source-rich updates;
- a measurable search query set;
- a refresh date.

### Newsletter

Operate one core product, provisionally **The Obscured Brief**, with a fixed structure:

- three developments or records worth understanding;
- one overlooked item;
- one chart/source/primary document;
- one Obscured Records feature;
- one clear invitation to read, reply or submit a source lead.

Begin at a cadence the editorial team can sustain. Do not claim confirmation, unsubscribe or sending behavior until the actual provider path is configured and verified end to end.

### Partnerships and syndication

Build a tracked relationship pipeline for:

- independent newsletters;
- student publications;
- research groups;
- podcasts;
- domain communities;
- university organizations;
- compatible independent publishers.

Every outreach row must distinguish: researched, contacted, replied, active, declined, or inactive. Do not treat a target list as a partnership list.

### Distribution package per story

For each approved article prepare:

- canonical URL;
- 40–70 word excerpt;
- one short social version;
- one longer professional-network version;
- newsletter blurb;
- source/visual card where rights allow;
- target communities/partners;
- UTM convention;
- seven-day and thirty-day review dates.

### Outputs by 30 November

- 8–12 live content clusters;
- one functioning newsletter editorial workflow;
- at least four weeks of distribution retrospectives;
- documented partner pipeline;
- repeatable story launch checklist;
- known top acquisition channels based on actual measurements rather than assumptions.

## Phase 3 — Authority and original value
### 1–31 December 2026

### Objectives

Move from “a site with articles” to “a publication known for a small number of distinctive recurring products.”

### Signature franchises

Develop three recurring formats:

1. **Obscured Deep Dives** — source-heavy, 2,000–5,000 word features or investigations where the evidence supports that depth.
2. **Obscured Conversations** — interviews with researchers, engineers, founders, historians, analysts and domain experts.
3. **Obscured 100** — a carefully defined annual/periodic index of overlooked people, systems, ideas, records or developments. Methodology must be published before any ranked form is used; an unranked curated index is the safer default.

### Source network

Create a structured source database with fields for:

- expertise;
- affiliation;
- public contact path;
- prior interaction;
- story relevance;
- consent/quotation notes;
- follow-up status.

Use sources for specific reporting questions. Do not mass-message people without a concrete editorial reason.

### Original data

Develop 1–3 small, defensible data products where provenance and update cadence are clear. Possibilities include archival timelines, incident databases, research trackers or source maps tied directly to published reporting.

### Outputs by 31 December

- at least two signature franchises operating;
- interview workflow and consent/quote-check procedure;
- a maintained source network;
- at least one original data or visual reporting product;
- documented evidence about which franchises generate returning readers, subscriptions or meaningful referrals.

## Phase 4 — Scale and monetization
### 1–31 January 2027

### Objectives

Add revenue and organizational scale only where editorial independence and reader trust remain explicit.

### Sponsorship

Create a simple inventory:

- newsletter sponsor;
- section sponsor;
- event sponsor;
- clearly labelled sponsored research/briefing only if the format and separation rules are explicit.

Editorial conclusions are not sold. Sponsored placement and editorial coverage must be separated and disclosed.

### Membership

Test a minimal membership proposition around support and useful access, not artificial paywall complexity. Potential benefits:

- ad-light/ad-free experience;
- early briefings;
- data downloads;
- member events;
- archive tools;
- contributor/community sessions.

### Research products and events

Pilot one high-quality research brief or data product and one virtual event. Treat these as experiments with explicit costs, conversion and retention measurements.

### Organization

By January, separate responsibility into:

- publishing/editorial;
- fact checking/research;
- visuals;
- newsletter;
- growth/distribution;
- engineering;
- partnerships/revenue.

A person may cover multiple functions, but every recurring function must have an accountable owner.

### Outputs by 31 January

- clear recurring editorial calendar;
- reliable publication and distribution workflow;
- measurable returning audience;
- owned newsletter audience;
- at least one monetization experiment with real conversion data;
- contributor/source network with explicit ownership;
- operating review that determines what to scale, stop or redesign.

## Weekly operating rhythm

### Monday — editorial
- choose the week’s priority stories;
- confirm editor/reviewer;
- set evidence and rights requirements;
- select one flagship;
- identify one archive/update opportunity.

### Wednesday — distribution
- review search/referral/direct/email performance;
- prepare launch packages;
- check partner pipeline;
- decide which stories need updates or stronger packaging.

### Friday — newsroom review
For each meaningful publication, record:

- views/sessions if analytics are enabled;
- engaged time;
- completion/scroll proxy if collected;
- source of visit;
- newsletter conversion;
- backlinks/referrals;
- corrections or reader feedback;
- whether the story should be updated, expanded, repackaged or left alone.

### Sunday — founder review
Answer:

1. What materially improved?
2. What failed or stalled?
3. Which stories earned repeat attention?
4. Which process consumed effort without improving quality or distribution?
5. What gets more resources next week?
6. What gets stopped?

## Core metrics

### Audience
- returning readers;
- direct traffic;
- search traffic;
- referral traffic;
- engaged reading time;
- repeat visits within 7/30 days.

### Owned audience
- newsletter pending captures;
- confirmed subscribers once a real confirmation flow exists;
- issue open/click/reply metrics only after the provider is real and verified;
- unsubscribe/complaint rate once sending exists.

### Editorial
- stories entering review;
- stories approved;
- stories published;
- percentage with complete source/rights checklist;
- corrections and material updates;
- average review cycle time.

### Distribution
- stories with complete launch package;
- referring domains;
- partner replies;
- active syndication/partner relationships;
- search impressions/clicks once Search Console or equivalent is connected.

### Business
- sponsor leads by state;
- sponsor revenue;
- member conversions;
- research/event revenue;
- revenue per thousand engaged visits only when both numerator and denominator are real.

---

## Stop conditions

Pause scaling if any of the following occurs:

- fact checking becomes superficial;
- unreviewed drafts are being pushed to meet cadence;
- rights/provenance warnings are ignored;
- distribution claims are being reported without evidence;
- editorial and sponsorship boundaries become ambiguous;
- the organization cannot respond to corrections or submissions;
- traffic growth materially degrades reader experience or site reliability.

The publication should become larger only when its operating discipline remains intact.
