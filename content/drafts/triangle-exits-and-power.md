A fire escape is an object. The ability to leave work safely is a relationship between people, rules and that object. Remembering the Triangle Waist Company only through the first of these risks losing the second.

On 25 March 1911, a fire at the New York garment factory killed 146 people. The U.S. Department of Labor places the disaster within the history of the New York Factory Investigating Commission and the struggle over workplace protection. Its account connects the fire to an investigation with consequences beyond a single building. That is the historical starting point for this essay, rather than an invitation to reconstruct a worker's final minutes without testimony.

## The door on the drawing

A floor plan can establish where a door was. It cannot, by itself, tell us whether a frightened person could reach it, whether it was usable, or what consequences a worker expected for approaching it during an ordinary day. Those are separate questions. Good history has to put the plan beside testimony and employment conditions.

The difference matters because the word access can conceal several tests. A route may exist, be marked, be physically clear and still not be available to everyone who needs it. An inspection concerned only with the presence of an exit can produce a reassuring inventory while missing the actual ability to escape. This is an analytical distinction, not a claim that every particular mechanism operated at Triangle.

The same caution applies to the familiar promise that a disaster changed everything. A reform has a date, a scope and an enforcement history. Passing a rule is a different event from making compliance ordinary. An account that ends with legislation can accidentally treat the work of inspectors, organizers and workers as a footnote to a completed rescue.

## Remembering without using the dead

There is a temptation to make victims serve as the opening illustration for a lesson aimed at managers. That arrangement gives the institution the final word. A more responsible account should leave room for the lives that safety reform did not restore, and for the people whose demands preceded public attention.

We should also resist lending a voice to the dead simply because a narrative needs one. A documented name, occupation or family relationship is more valuable than an invented thought at a window. The limits of the archive belong in the writing.

Triangle remains useful to think with precisely because an exit is so concrete. It allows a reader to ask an equally concrete question of any institution: who can identify an unsafe condition, who can refuse it, and who has the power to make the refusal effective? A door is necessary. It is not the whole answer.
