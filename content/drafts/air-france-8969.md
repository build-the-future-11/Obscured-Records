Before French forces intervened aboard Air France Flight 8969 in Marseille, GIGN personnel had rehearsed with an aircraft of the same type at Palma de Mallorca. The French gendarmerie's retrospective places that preparation alongside observation and negotiation before the rescue on 26 December 1994.

The aircraft had been seized in Algiers two days earlier by four armed men. Three hostages were killed before departure. In Marseille, the remaining hostages were evacuated and the four hijackers were killed. The cabin remained the site of the crisis, but the move changed the authority and resources outside it.

## The difference between an objective and an outcome

A hijacking creates several histories at once. There is the experience of those confined in the aircraft, the work of negotiators and responders, and the later effort to establish the attackers' purpose. An account can move between them, but it should not give each the same evidentiary status.

The documented outcome does not settle every later account of the attackers' intentions. Those claims require attributable evidence separate from the chronology of seizure, negotiation and intervention.

The aircraft also changes the meaning of location. Passengers may remain in the same seats while jurisdiction, available resources and the surrounding operational conditions change. A route map helps explain those transitions, provided that the writer does not use it as a substitute for a precise chronology.

Television images preserve visible action, not the content of every negotiation or the experience of everyone confined in the cabin.

## Preparation outside the televised moment

The gendarmerie's account describes GIGN personnel preparing with an aircraft of the same type at Palma de Mallorca before arriving in Marseille. It also describes observation of the seized aircraft before intervention. The visible assault was therefore preceded by work that the television images of the final encounter cannot encompass.

Preparation concerns a range of practical questions: the aircraft's configuration, possible approaches and the conditions under which a plan could be carried out. This does not mean a rehearsal reproduces a hostage situation exactly. It means that some features can be examined before people must act amid uncertainty.

Location changed the available response. While the aircraft remained in Algeria, the French force could not simply treat it as an operation on French soil. In Marseille, the surrounding authority and resources were different even though the people inside were still confined to an aircraft. A route is also a history of changing jurisdiction.

The assault was preceded by rehearsal, observation and negotiation in the gendarmerie's account. Those reported preparations are a defined part of the history. Independent reporting and hostage testimony are still needed to describe the wider decisions and the experience inside the aircraft.
