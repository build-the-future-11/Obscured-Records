Temporary is a description of intention. It says how long someone hopes an arrangement will last. It says nothing, by itself, about how much force the arrangement must withstand while it exists.

Before the 1974 explosion at Flixborough, a damaged reactor was removed and a bypass connected the neighboring reactors. HSE's account identifies inadequate assessment and testing of the modification among the failings. The explosion killed 28 workers. The agency's summary also retains uncertainty about aspects of the initiating sequence. A responsible retelling should not eliminate that uncertainty to make the bypass into an excessively neat single cause.

## Production through a changed machine

The appeal of a bypass is straightforward: part of a system is unavailable, so another connection allows work to continue. Yet once material flows through the new connection, that connection is part of the operational plant. It is no longer merely a proposal sketched beside the original design.

This observation changes the relevant comparison. The question is not whether the modification seems smaller than the equipment it replaces. It is whether the modified system has been understood under the conditions it will actually experience. Shape, support, movement and interaction with neighboring components can matter even when the purpose of the connection sounds simple.

Documentation is often treated as something added after engineering. In a change process, a drawing can instead be a tool for making an assumption available to other people. Without a shared representation, a reviewer may be approving a different mental picture from the one a fabricator or operator has in mind.

None of this proves that more paperwork alone produces safety. Documents can be wrong, unread or treated as ceremonial. Their value lies in the questions they force into the open and the disagreements they allow someone to resolve before operation.

## The lifespan of an exception

An exception can acquire the familiarity of a normal arrangement simply by remaining in use. Every uneventful day then becomes tempting evidence that it is acceptable. But uneventful operation may establish only that a particular combination of stresses has not yet occurred.

The management question is therefore not just who authorized the change. It is who knows it is still there, who understands the conditions attached to approval, and what would require it to be reconsidered. Temporary work needs an owner through its full lifetime, including removal or formal incorporation into the design.

Flixborough offers no comfort to the idea that the real plant is the original plant on the drawing. The real plant is the one carrying material today. Its improvised or transitional parts participate fully in its hazards. Calling a connection temporary cannot reduce the consequences of getting it wrong.
