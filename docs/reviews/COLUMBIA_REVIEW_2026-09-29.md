## Findings supported by the CAIB archive

- The foam strike was seen during review of launch imagery; no conclusive image of the resulting wing damage existed.
- The Debris Assessment Team discussed on-orbit imagery and tasked its NASA co-chair with requesting it.
- CAIB found the request moved through an engineering route rather than the mission operational route, diluting its urgency.
- CAIB recorded three independent imagery requests.
- It found substantial uncertainty in the damage assessment and that assumptions/uncertainties were not fully conveyed upward.
- It found managers focused on who was requesting imagery and that the operational chain lacked knowledge/clearance to understand national imaging capabilities.
- It found program managers effectively placed the burden on engineers to prove the strike was unsafe rather than establish that it was safe.

## Editorial consequence

The original draft's core premise was sound but too abstract. The revision anchors the argument in explicit CAIB findings and replaces the vague idea of “choosing not to seek evidence” with the documented problem of routing, ownership, uncertainty and decision authority.

## Counterfactual boundary

The revised article does not claim that imagery would have guaranteed a repair, rescue or survival. Additional imagery changes the information state; it does not by itself prove a successful intervention was available.
